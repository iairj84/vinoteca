Vinoteca 1.7.6

Wine cellar notebook with an AI sommelier.

Download the app

  2. Open the fileFrom the download notification, or Files → Downloads.

New in 1.7.6: two readers, either can carry the scan. If the structured read fails the line read answers, and the other way round, so one unreadable reply no longer costs the whole photo. Wine lists read as lines, not JSON. One printed wine per line with its glass and bottle price, so a long list can no longer arrive unreadable. Also: scans that finish. One image per read leaves room for the answer, so a long wine list is no longer cut off mid-JSON; a truncated answer is salvaged rather than thrown away, and a single bottle gets a small-print pass for its ABV. Also: wine lists actually read. Every wine on a restaurant list is listed with its glass and bottle price; if a legible list comes back empty the app retries it automatically. Also: slower, sharper scans. Larger photos, low reasoning, self-checked vintages and ABVs, and a To be sure box that says what to re-shoot. One shutter, and the verdict everywhere, as in 1.7.1: the three scan chips are gone — the photo already says whether it is a single bottle, a shelf or a wine list, and the app now asks the model in the same reply that reads the bottles. That also fixed a quiet bug: a wine list scanned under “shop shelf” had its list prices judged against a retail price band. A single bottle photographed now gets the same Buy it / Worth a look / Leave it verdict as a shelf, with its reasons, which is the ordinary shop moment.

From 1.7.0: a verdict where you actually decide. The 1–5 match line is gone from bottles you already own — it ranked a shelf you had already paid for, and in a cellar of similar reds it said the same two things about everything. A bottle page now shows its facts and a Your rules card: under your ABV ceiling, over it, or not on the label, one line per gate. The score moved to the shop: scan a shelf and each bottle gets Buy it / Worth a look / Leave it with the reasons — your ceiling, sweetness and oak, your price band, whether you already own it, and the gap it fills in the cellar. There is a new At a restaurant mode that reads a wine list or the bottle the sommelier brought over, judges it against your limits and ignores the price band, because a list price carries a markup. Shelf scans now read the ABV off the label, and a reference-note lookup records the ABV a page prints when the bottle has none — the one hard limit the app can check, and the reason a shop scan is worth more than a cellar page.

From 1.6.6: the note reader stopped saving non-answers. A reply that described the wine and then admitted tasting notes were not available used to be stored as a reference note, under a critic's authority; it now counts as a miss and says so.

From 1.6.5: every bottle gets a match line, with a stored score shown even when its reason is empty.

From 1.6.4: the note reader accepts what models actually write. The check asked for JSON and a stripped-down request came back as prose, so every bottle failed with “the model answered without the notes I asked for” and nothing was saved. It now asks for five plain lines — MATCH, NOTE, TAGS, SOURCES, CAVEAT — still reads JSON when a model offers it, and if an answer cannot be read at all it shows the first 200 characters of it in the card

From 1.6.3: the lookup actually looks things up. Gemini's Google Search grounding turned out to be a paid feature — a free key answers “quota exceeded” with a limit of zero, so every check stopped before reading a single page. The app now searches and fetches the pages itself: Falstaff, Sommelier's Window, the producer's own vintage notes, Vivino, and it asks the model only to report what those pages say, keeping the links on the bottle. Either provider works; no extra key.

New in 1.6.2: the whole cellar, checked. Settings → Reference notes walks every bottle that has not been checked yet: one request per bottle at your key's pace, each note saved with the pages it came from, and a run you can stop and start again without repeating a bottle. With 100+ bottles that is about ten minutes on a free Gemini key instead of a hundred taps. Ask also receives up to 140 bottles on Gemini now (still 55 on Groq, whose minute budget is smaller), so the rest of the cellar is no longer invisible to it.

From 1.6.1: reference notes you can check. Each bottle page has a Look up tasting notes button: Gemini reads published notes with Google Search and saves them on the bottle, with the pages it used listed underneath. Until a note carries sources it is general knowledge, not fact — Ask is no longer allowed to invent a bottle's oak, acidity or fruit from its name. A plan request (a meal, a day, three bottles) now comes back in a fixed shape: what to open, when to chill or decant, what it is for, and why, in plain text.

Still there from 1.6: mytaste.md is a receipt. Every value carries the words you chose and a confidence — Yours, Ours, Guess or Unknown — and a field with no source reads as unknown instead of being filled in. The file opens with those rules, then your anchor line, the taste axes, the gates the app checks, a settings table, the pairing policy, house rules, who you drink with, and two honesty sections: what the file doesn't know, and any conflict between what you said and what you pick. It stores nothing about which grape suits which dish — pairing is worked out per dish when you ask.

Still there from 1.5: the ten-tap quick start that writes the file in about two minutes, the gates checked by the app itself (a missing ABV reads as unknown, never as a pass), the three styles to look for instead of a "profile saved" screen, and Settings → Advanced → Edit mytaste.md for anyone who would rather write it.

Updating from 1.0–1.5: install straight over the old app, your cellar, notes and taste file are kept. Open Settings → Backup and import → Export backup once — a backup is a single file you can keep off the phone.

Also in from earlier versions: Google Gemini next to Groq, each with its own key, model ids and pace, and per-provider Test connection; Ask sends the whole mytaste.md (1.4.1 fixed a 2,600-character cut that had been dropping the pairing table), and batch shelf scans read three to five bottles per photo.

It is a sideloaded build, not from the Play Store, so Play Protect will warn you — expected. The app has no trackers and no account. Your cellar, notes and mytaste.md live on the phone; only photos you analyse and the text you send to the model are sent to the provider you configured, with your own key.
